Three ways we lost leads we had already paid for.

One

The seller called someone else back first.

Three VAs means three calls at once, at best. When six leads came in together, the rest sat in a queue, sometimes for hours. A motivated seller does not fill in one form. By the time we rang, they had already talked to a buyer who rang first, and that buyer set the price.

Two

Our best leads arrived when the office was empty.

People fill in forms after work. Evenings, late nights, Sunday mornings. Our VAs worked US business hours, so 53% of our leads landed when nobody was there. Those leads got their first call the next morning, cold, from a company they had half forgotten contacting.

Three

Follow-up quietly stopped at attempt two.

Nobody decided to give up on a lead. There was just always a newer one to call. Attempt five never happened. A lot of our deals come from attempt five, which means we were throwing away leads that were still worth money, and calling them dead in the CRM.

So we put Sloan in front of the phone.

Sloan is our own voice agent. It makes the first call, asks our standard questions, and sends the transcript and the answers to our team. That is the whole job.

It calls in seconds.

The seller is often still on our website when the phone rings. You are the first buyer they speak to, not the fourth.

It calls everyone at once.

Six leads arrive together, six calls go out together. No queue, and no call-back landing on a busy line.

It never clocks off.

No shift, no breaks, no holidays. We still do not ring phones in the middle of the night, so an overnight lead gets a text straight away and a call first thing.

It keeps trying.

Up to ten attempts on every lead, on schedule, whether anyone remembers or not. Attempt five happens every time now.

Now our team only talks to sellers who picked up.

This is the change we felt most.

Sloan does the dialing. It takes the ringing out, the voicemails, the dead numbers. It also handles most of the screening on its own, so a lot of leads are already qualified before a person is involved.

Our team no longer spends half the day listening to phones ring. They spend it talking to sellers who answered. When a call needs a human, they take it.

We did not replace our VAs. We deleted the four hours a day they spent waiting for someone to pick up.

Same leads. Same questions. Same team. One thing changed.

We did not touch lead sources or ad spend when we switched. Only who made the first call.

We pay a VA for the month. We pay Sloan for the talking.

A VA costs the same whether the phone is ringing or not. So if only about half the shift is a real conversation, an hour of actual talking costs roughly double what it looks like on the invoice.

Sloan costs money only while a call is connected. Idle time is free. That is the whole difference, and it is a difference in shape rather than in rate.

One more thing. If you want an American voice on the phone, a US based caller costs considerably more than an offshore one. On most calls, sellers cannot tell Sloan is not one.

What Sloan is bad at.

You would find all of this out in week one, so here it is now. If any of it is a dealbreaker, better you know at this point.

Emotional calls need a person.

Probate, divorce, foreclosure. Sloan asks the next question. A good VA knows when to stop asking.

It cannot follow a hunch.

A seller drops a hint that they are more motivated than they said. A sharp caller chases it. Sloan carries on down the script. This is the one we feel most.

Some sellers can tell.

Most cannot. A few can, and a few of those hang up. That is a real loss, and it is smaller than the voicemail they used to get on a Saturday.

Odd answers break the call.

If a seller answers sideways, the call can end half finished. It gets flagged and a person picks it up. Nobody is standing by to rescue it live.

Transcripts make mistakes.

Addresses and figures need checking before they go anywhere near an offer.

When it breaks, it breaks everywhere.

One VA having a bad day costs you one seat. One wrong setting costs you every lead until somebody notices. We check the dashboard every morning.

Sloan does not negotiate, make offers, book appointments or close. That is still your team, and if your team is not ready to take the handoff, faster qualification will not help you.
